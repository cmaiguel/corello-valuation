# Corello Investor Q&A — Extended Objection Handling

**Confidential | Seed Round Preparation**

---

## How to Use This Document

This is the extended Q&A playbook for investor meetings. Each entry includes the investor's underlying concern, the recommended response, and what NOT to say.

For the short-form version, see `corello_founder_talking_points.md`.

---

## Revenue & Traction Questions

---

### "Is this $700K ARR?"

**Underlying concern:** Investors want to know if this is recurring, predictable software revenue (ARR) or one-time / project-based revenue.

**Say:**
> "No. $700K is signed contracted revenue — customer commitments in executed contracts. It is not ARR. Some of this will convert to recurring software revenue as deployments go live. We are not presenting this as an ARR multiple."

**Do not say:**
> - "It's basically ARR" — It is not.
> - "You can think of it as run-rate" — You cannot.

---

### "What's your gross margin?"

**Underlying concern:** AI companies often have low margins due to inference costs. Investors want to know if the business model is sustainable.

**Say:**
> "We are still building out the full gross margin picture. Our software component has high structural margin potential — we do not have significant human-in-the-loop delivery costs. AI inference costs are real and we're tracking them. We will have a clean gross margin breakdown before our Series A."

**If you have the data:**
> "Our software subscription margin is approximately X%. Implementation services margin is lower at Y%, which is typical. Blended gross margin is Z%."

---

### "When will you have ARR?"

**Underlying concern:** Investors want to know your path to predictable recurring revenue, which is the foundation for Series A metrics.

**Say:**
> "Our contracted customers are in deployment. As contracts go live and billing begins, the software subscription component converts to ARR. Our target is $[X] in ARR by [month/quarter], which is the primary milestone this raise funds."

---

### "How concentrated is your revenue?"

**Underlying concern:** If one customer is 80%+ of $700K, losing that customer destroys the traction story.

**Say:**
> "We have [N] contracted customers. Our largest customer represents [X]% of contracted revenue. We're deliberately building a diversified base — no single customer represents more than [Y]% of our target ARR base."

## Valuation Questions

---

### "How did you get to $12M pre-money?"

**Say:**
> "We used a blended methodology: comparable Seed round sizes in industrial AI ($5M–$17M raises), standard Seed ownership mechanics at 15–20%, and a sanity-check on our contracted revenue multiple. At $12M pre, investors take ~17.2% ownership on a $2.5M raise — that's squarely in the institutional Seed range. We're not using an ARR multiple because we don't have ARR yet."

---

### "That's a high multiple on $700K."

**Say:**
> "17x on contracted revenue is the sanity check we ran, but it's not the basis of the valuation — and contracted revenue is not ARR. Seed valuations in this category are driven by team, market, and early traction evidence, not revenue multiples. The industrial AI comp set shows Seed rounds at $5M–$17M. Our $12M pre is on the conservative end of that range."

---

### "Can we do this at $8M–$10M pre?"

**Say:**
> "We're anchoring at $12M. At $10M pre, your ownership goes to 20% on a $2.5M raise — that's at the high end of institutional Seed dilution for a $2.5M check. We think $12M is fair given the traction we're bringing in. If you have a strong conviction reason to be lower, let's talk — but we'd want to understand what would change your view."

**Founder anchor response:**
> "Our anchor is actually $14M, which gets you to 15.15% — still excellent Seed ownership. We opened at $12M because we think it's fair and we want aligned, happy investors."

## Market Questions

---

### "The manufacturing market is slow to adopt AI."

**Say:**
> "That's historically true. But we're seeing a meaningful shift — manufacturers are under margin pressure from labor costs and supply chain volatility, and they're now willing to invest in AI tools that have a clear ROI. Our $700K in contracts is evidence that the willingness to pay is here now, not in the future."

---

### "Who are your competitors?"

**Say:**
> "At the platform level, some competitors include [names]. But our differentiation is [specific technical or go-to-market advantage]. We're not trying to be a general-purpose manufacturing SaaS — we're focused on [specific use case], where we have deep expertise and a head start."

---

## Team & Execution Questions

---

### "Can this small team execute?"

**Say:**
> "This raise is specifically to build the team. We've validated product-market fit with $700K in contracts with a lean team — that's capital efficiency. Post-raise, we're hiring [N] engineers, a sales hire, and a CS hire. The $12M pre reflects the current stage; the $2.5M funds the scaling."

---

### "Why haven't you raised more?"

**Say:**
> "We raised exactly what we need to hit the next set of milestones: convert contracted revenue into ARR, build the repeatable deployment playbook, and establish the metrics for a Series A. Raising more than you need at Seed dilutes founders unnecessarily and often leads to undisciplined spending."

## Diligence Questions

---

### "Can we see the contracts?"

**Say:**
> "Absolutely — that's standard diligence. We'll share redacted copies under NDA. The contracts confirm the revenue commitments."

**Prepare:** Have redacted (customer name and identifying info removed, all financial terms visible) copies ready. Work with your attorney to determine what can be shared.

---

### "Can we talk to a customer?"

**Say:**
> "Yes. We have [N] customers willing to serve as references. We'll connect you after we've signed a term sheet."

**Prepare:** Have 2–3 customers pre-briefed and willing to take reference calls. Brief them on what investors typically ask (product value, deployment experience, likelihood to expand).
