## 1. Executive Summary

Corello is raising a $2.5M Seed round. The recommended pre-money valuation is **$12.0M**, implying a $14.5M post-money valuation and approximately **17.24% new investor ownership**.

The valuation is supported by $700K in signed contracted revenue — real customer commitments in manufacturing — and is consistent with early-stage industrial AI market context. It is not priced as a mature SaaS ARR valuation.

**Bottom line:** $12M pre-money is the cleanest, most defensible ask. It reflects disciplined Seed ownership math, validates contracted demand without overstating revenue quality, and leaves upside for investors as contracts convert to repeatable ARR.

## 6. Valuation Methodologies

### 6.1 Seed Ownership Method (Weight: 35%)

At Seed, investors typically target 15–25% ownership. Common benchmarks:

| Target Ownership | Implied Pre-Money | Classification |
|-----------------|-------------------|----------------|
| 20.0% | $10.0M | Investor-friendly |
| 17.24% | **$12.0M** | **Base (Recommended)** |
| 15.15% | $14.2M | Founder-friendly |

**Conclusion:** Ownership method supports $10M–$14.2M. $12M is the base.

### 6.2 Contracted Revenue Multiple Method (Weight: 30%)

Because $700K is contracted revenue (not ARR), this method is a **sanity check only**, not a primary valuation driver. SaaS ARR multiples (15x–30x) do not apply directly.

For context:
- $12M pre = 17.1x contracted revenue
- $13M pre = 18.6x contracted revenue
- $14M pre = 20.0x contracted revenue

At early Seed, pre-money multiples of 15x–20x contracted (non-recurring) revenue are high but can be defensible for AI-native companies with strong demand signals. $12M sits at the disciplined end of this range.

**Conclusion:** Revenue method supports $10M–$13M pre. $14M is an aggressive but potentially defensible anchor.

### 6.3 Benchmark Round-Size Method (Weight: 20%)

Corello's $2.5M raise is smaller than most industrial AI Seed rounds ($5M–$17M). Raising less does not mechanically imply a lower valuation, but it suggests modesty is appropriate.

If Corello raised $5M at standard 20% dilution, pre-money would be $20M — clearly too high for this stage. At $2.5M, $12M pre (17.24% dilution) is disciplined.

**Conclusion:** Benchmark method supports $10M–$13M pre for a $2.5M raise.

### 6.4 ARR-Conversion Potential Method (Weight: 15%)

If contracted revenue converts to $700K+ ARR within 12–18 months, and the company achieves 2x ARR growth in year one, a $14M–$15M pre would become retrospectively defensible at a Series A step-up.

This method is **conditional** and should not be the primary basis for valuation today.

**Conclusion:** Supports $14M–$15M pre only if conversion happens. Not yet proven.

### 6.5 Blended Conclusion

| Method | Weight | Supported Range |
|--------|--------|-----------------|

## 10. Risks and Haircuts

The following factors could support a lower valuation:

1. **Services-heavy contracts.** If $700K includes significant implementation, consulting, or professional services, the software revenue base is smaller. Haircut: –$1M–$2M pre.
2. **Short contract durations or cancellation clauses.** Contracts with easy exit provisions reduce the strength of the revenue signal.
3. **High customer concentration.** If 1–2 customers represent >60% of contracted revenue, the base is fragile.
4. **Limited gross margin visibility.** If AI inference, implementation, and support costs are unknown, margins could be low.
5. **No ARR conversion proof yet.** The conversion from contracted revenue to recurring ARR has not been demonstrated.

Investors will ask about all of these. Prepare specific answers for each.

## 13. Final Recommendation

Corello should pitch a **$2.5M Seed raise at a $12M pre-money valuation**. This implies a $14.5M post-money valuation and approximately **17.24% new investor ownership**.

The valuation is defensible because Corello has $700K in signed contracted revenue and operates in a high-interest industrial AI market. However, because the $700K is contracted revenue rather than ARR, Corello should avoid presenting the round as an ARR-multiple-based SaaS valuation.

The cleanest positioning is that $12M pre-money is a **disciplined Seed valuation** that gives investors meaningful ownership while giving the company enough capital to convert contracted demand into repeatable ARR.

**If investors push back on valuation:**
- Floor: $10M pre (20% investor ownership) — still respectable
- Walk-away: below $10M pre is not justified by contracted demand and market context
- Stretch: $14M pre is a defensible founder anchor if contract quality is strong, but should not be the opening ask

**If investors ask about structure:**
- A priced round at $12M pre is preferred
- A post-money SAFE at $14.5M cap is economically equivalent and acceptable
- Avoid uncapped SAFEs or SAFEs with onerous MFN provisions
