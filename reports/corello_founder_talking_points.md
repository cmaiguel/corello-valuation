# Corello Founder Talking Points
**Seed Round — $2.5M at $12M Pre-Money**
*Confidential — For Founder Use Only*

---

## The Headline Numbers

- **Raising:** $2.5M
- **Pre-money:** $12.0M
- **Post-money:** $14.5M
- **Investor ownership:** ~17.2%
- **Contracted revenue:** $700K (signed commitments, not ARR)

---

## 30-Second Answer: "What are you raising?"

> "We're raising $2.5M at a $12M pre-money valuation. We have $700K in signed contracted revenue — not ARR. We believe $12M pre is a disciplined Seed valuation that reflects real customer validation while giving new investors approximately 17.2% ownership."

**Use this when:** Investor asks the opening question in the first meeting.

---

## 60-Second Answer: "Why $12M pre-money?"

> "We are still early, so we are not pricing Corello like a mature ARR SaaS company. The valuation reflects signed contracted demand, our vertical AI position in manufacturing, and the amount of ownership investors receive in a $2.5M Seed.
>
> At $12M pre, the post-money is $14.5M, so investors own roughly 17.2% — that's a fair Seed outcome for a company with validated demand.
>
> We're not claiming an AI premium. We're saying: we have $700K in customer commitments in a large underserved market, and $12M pre is the disciplined, credible ask."

**Use this when:** Investor pushes on valuation rationale in the first 10 minutes.

---

## If Investor Says: "That's Not ARR."

> "That's right, and we are being very clear about that. We are not claiming $700K ARR. We are saying $700K of contracted revenue validates urgent customer demand. This round is about converting that demand into repeatable deployments and ARR.
>
> We want investors who understand the distinction — and who want to be involved early, before contracted revenue becomes proven ARR and the valuation step-up has already happened."

**Key move:** Flip it. Being honest about it is a sign of credibility, not weakness. Investors who understand the difference are the right investors.

---

## If Investor Says: "Valuation Is Too High."

> "I understand. We are trying to be disciplined — $12M pre is already below what you'd see for a company with equivalent ARR.
>
> At $12M pre, investors get 17.2% ownership, which is meaningful for a Seed round.
>
> If there's a specific concern around contract quality or ARR conversion risk, I'd rather talk through that directly. We can discuss structure — milestone tranches, pro rata rights, lead allocation — but we believe the headline valuation is reasonable given what we have."

**Key move:** Do not immediately drop the price. Understand what the objection is actually about — contract quality, market risk, or just negotiation — and address the root cause.

---

## If Investor Asks: "Would You Take $10M Pre?"

> "We would consider the full package: check size, speed, strategic value, pro rata rights, board involvement, and ability to help us win manufacturing customers. Our target is $12M pre, but we are optimizing for the right partner, not just the last dollar of valuation.
>
> If you're a strong lead with a $1M+ check, sector expertise, and genuine manufacturing customer introductions, we'd have that conversation. But the headline number matters too — $10M pre dilutes our team more than the demand signal justifies."

**Key move:** Don't say no. Say: the full package matters. Keep the door open for the right investor.

---

## If Investor Asks: "Would You Take a SAFE?"

> "We can consider a SAFE if it's clean and founder-friendly. A $14.5M post-money SAFE cap is economically similar to a $12M pre-money priced round with a $2.5M raise — same dilution, same entry price.
>
> We'd want to ensure: no MFN if we do multiple SAFEs, a clean conversion mechanism, and alignment on pro rata rights. If those terms are founder-standard, a SAFE is acceptable."

**Key move:** SAFE is fine. But make sure the cap is right and the terms are clean.

---

## Negotiation Posture

**Opening ask:** $12M pre-money  
**Walk-away floor:** $10M pre-money (20% investor ownership — still defensible)  
**True anchor:** $14M pre if a strategic lead engages with strong conviction  
**Avoid:** Pricing below $10M — it undervalues the contracted demand and sets a low Series A baseline  
**Avoid:** Pricing above $15M pre without proven recurring ARR — it invites skepticism and longer diligence  

### What to optimize for beyond valuation:
1. Check size — a $750K+ lead check signals conviction
2. Pro rata rights — protect your equity at Series A
3. Speed — minimize time in market; a long fundraise drains the team
4. Strategic value — manufacturing customer intros, operator network, distribution
5. Board seat preference — consider whether a board seat is worth it at this stage

---

## The One Thing to Repeat in Every Meeting

> "$700K in signed contracted revenue is not ARR. We know that. We are pricing this round to reflect early-stage traction in a large market — not to claim we're a mature SaaS company. $12M pre is the honest, credible ask."
